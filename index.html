Not another screen-time app

You already know your screen time. So what?

A weekly report doesn't tell you what to do. A blocker just gets in your way until you delete it. SelfLabs does one thing neither can: it connects what you did to how you felt.

Blocking.

Open whatever you want, whenever you want. SelfLabs never stands between you and an app.

Lectures.

No "you should sleep more." Just your own days, side by side. You decide what to do about it.

Homework.

No journaling, no typing. One tap in the morning, two at night. That's the whole job.

How it works

Try one small thing.
See what changes.

01 · Evening

One small thing at a time.

A 20-minute walk. Phone across the room by 11:30. Calling a friend instead of scrolling. Small enough to say yes to. Some days you try it, some days you don't, so there's something to compare.

02 · Night

Two taps.
Done.

How in control did today feel? Did you do the thing? Meanwhile your phone quietly notes the basics: sleep, pickups, when it went down for the night.

03 · A few days later

Cause, meet effect.

Every dot is one of your days. When a pattern shows up, you see it, along with how sure we are. "Early signal" means exactly that.

Mornings

A check-in you can do with your eyes half open.

One tap. Then a 20-second reaction game, scored against your own usual. It's hard to fake, and more fun than a questionnaire.

Day 14

Your playbook.

After two weeks you get a short list nobody else has: the things that work for you, tested by you. Including the stuff that turned out not to matter.

Your friend who's fine on six hours of sleep? Good for them. This one's yours.

Lab partners

Better with friends.

Race your friends on the morning game. Keep each other's streaks alive. They see your score, never your check-ins.

Privacy

Here's the deal.

SelfLabs sees

  • Which apps, and for how long
  • When your phone goes down at night
  • Sleep and steps, if you say yes

SelfLabs never sees

  • Messages, photos, anything you type
  • What you watch, post or search
  • Your grades

It stays on your phone. Not your parents'. Not your school's. Yours.
